Why Is My Refund Delayed? Common IRS Audit Flags and Processing Holds

A delayed refund can have several causes, including PATH Act timing, an IRS review of income or credits, or an identity check. A delay or hold by itself does not tell you that you are being audited or that the IRS found a problem.

How to check your refund status (and what each message means)

Use the IRS’s Where’s My Refund? tool on IRS.gov or in the free IRS2Go app. You can start checking about 24 hours after you e-file this year’s return, or about 4 weeks after you mail a paper return. Have three things ready: your Social Security number or ITIN, your filing status, and the exact whole-dollar refund amount on your return.

  • Return Received: the IRS has your return and is processing it. Most refunds are issued in less than 21 days for e-filed returns with direct deposit, but some take longer.
  • Refund Approved: your refund is approved, and the tool should show a date it will be sent.
  • Refund Sent: the IRS sent your money. A direct deposit can take up to 5 days to show in your account; a paper check can take several weeks by mail.

The tool updates once a day, usually overnight, so checking more often won’t show anything new. Only call the IRS about your refund if it has been 21 days or more since you e-filed, six weeks or more since you mailed your return, or the tool tells you to call.

PATH Act: EITC and ACTC refund timing

By law, the IRS cannot issue refunds before mid-February when a return claims the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) or Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC). This hold applies to the entire refund, not only the part connected to those credits. The IRS’s refund tracker can provide a personalized status and date when one is available.

Income verification and return reviews

The IRS may need more time to verify income, withholding, credits, or business income reported on a return. A CP05 notice generally says the IRS needs time to review information; it does not necessarily ask you to send documents. A CP05A or CP05B may request supporting information. Read the notice you received and follow its specific instructions and deadlines.

A review or refund hold is sometimes described online as an “audit flag,” but the delay alone does not establish that you are being audited. The IRS says a return review does not mean that you made an error or were dishonest.

Identity checks and return verification

If the IRS needs to confirm your identity or verify that you filed the return, it may send a letter such as CP5071 or 4883C. Use the verification method and contact details in your official IRS letter. If the letter directs you to verify online, go directly to IRS.gov and use its Verify Your Return service. A 4883C letter may ask you to call the number printed on the letter.

After you verify, the IRS says processing may take additional time. Keep the notice and requested records available, and check the IRS refund tracker for updates.

What you can do while you wait

  • Check Where’s My Refund? or your IRS Online Account for current status.
  • Read any IRS notice and follow its response deadline; don’t assume every delay needs a response.
  • Check that your bank account information is correct and allow time for your financial institution to post a payment.
  • Use IRS.gov directly for updates. Do not send tax documents or Social Security numbers through D-XIRE’s waitlist or free-resource forms.
Important: Refund timing depends on the return, IRS processing, notices, and financial institution. This information is general and does not determine what caused a specific refund delay.
