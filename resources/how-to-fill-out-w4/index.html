How to Fill Out a W-4 (and When to Update It)

A Form W-4 tells your employer how much federal income tax to take out of each paycheck. Most people only need Steps 1 and 5. If you have two jobs, kids, side income, tips or overtime, the other steps keep you from owing at tax time or having too much taken out.

By LaCaya Pope, IRS PTIN P03232493 · Updated November 2026

How to fill out the 2026 Form W-4, step by step

Step 1: Your info and filing status

Enter your name, address and Social Security number, then check the filing status you expect to use on your tax return: Single (or Married Filing Separately), Married Filing Jointly, or Head of Household. Everyone fills out Step 1.

Step 2: Two jobs, or your spouse works

Skip this if you have one job and aren’t married filing jointly with a working spouse. Otherwise, pick one option: use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator (most accurate), use the Multiple Jobs Worksheet on page 3, or check the box in Step 2(c) if there are only two jobs with similar pay. If you check 2(c), check it on both W-4s.

Step 3: Kids and other dependents

On line 3(a), multiply your children under 17 by $2,200. On line 3(b), multiply other dependents by $500. Add them for the Step 3 total. This only applies if your income will be $200,000 or less ($400,000 married filing jointly). With two jobs, claim dependents on the higher-paying job’s W-4 only.

Step 4(a): Other income

Income that has no tax taken out, like interest or a small side gig. Entering it here makes your job withhold more. If you’d rather not list it, you can use Step 4(c) instead.

Step 4(b): Deductions (new lines for 2026)

Use the Deductions Worksheet on page 4. For 2026 it has new lines for qualified tips, qualified overtime, car loan interest and the age 65+ deduction. If you earn tips or overtime, this is how you keep more of each paycheck now instead of waiting for a refund.

Step 4(c): Extra withholding

An extra dollar amount taken out of every paycheck. This is the easiest fix if you owed last April or have side income. My free withholding calculator tells you exactly what number to write here.

“Exempt from withholding” checkbox

New for 2026: instead of writing “Exempt,” there’s a checkbox. Only check it if you owed no federal income tax last year and expect to owe none this year. It expires each year, so you need a new W-4 by mid-February to stay exempt. Claiming exempt when you don’t qualify usually means a big bill at tax time.

Step 5: Sign and turn it in

Sign and date the form, then give it to your employer’s payroll or HR department, not the IRS. Check your next one or two pay stubs to make sure the change took effect.

When to update your W-4

Life changes

You got married or divorced, had a baby, or a child aged out of the credit (turned 17). Your spouse started or stopped working.

Job changes

You started a new job, picked up a second job or side gig, got a big raise, or started earning tips or overtime.

Your last tax return surprised you

You owed money last April, or you got a huge refund and would rather have that money in your paychecks during the year.

Do it before your last paychecks of the year: the fewer paychecks left, the bigger each adjustment has to be. Run D-XIRE’s Tax Withholding Calculator with your latest pay stub, then put the amount it gives you on Step 4(c). Not sure how to read your pay stub? See How to Read Your Pay Stub.
