How to Read Your Pay Stub: YTD Pay, Federal Tax and Pre-Tax Deductions

Your pay stub shows two columns that matter most for taxes: this paycheck (“Current”) and the year so far (“YTD”). Gross pay is what you earned before anything comes out; net pay is what lands in your account after taxes and deductions. Here’s what every other line means.

By LaCaya Pope, IRS PTIN P03232493 · Updated November 2026

Common pay stub labels, decoded

On your pay stubWhat it means
Gross pay / Total earningsEverything you earned before anything comes out: regular hours, overtime, tips, bonuses.
Regular / Overtime (OT) / TipsHow your gross pay breaks down. Overtime and tips matter for the new 2026 deductions.
Federal income tax (FIT, FITW, Fed W/H)Federal income tax taken out. This is the number that decides your refund or balance due.
Social Security (OASDI, SS)6.2% of your pay, up to a yearly limit ($184,500 for 2026).
Medicare (MED, MEDI)1.45% of your pay. An extra 0.9% applies above $200,000 of wages.
State and local tax (SIT, SWT, LIT)State or city income tax, if your state has one. Some states also take disability insurance (SDI).
Pre-tax deductionsTraditional 401(k), health, dental and vision premiums, HSA or FSA. These lower your taxable wages.
After-tax deductionsRoth 401(k), union dues, garnishments, some insurance. These don’t lower your taxable wages.
Net payYour take-home pay: what actually lands in your bank account.

What to look at for taxes

Current vs. YTD

“Current” is this paycheck only. “YTD” (year to date) is everything since January 1. For tax planning, YTD is the column that matters, because your tax is based on the whole year.

Gross pay vs. taxable wages

Your taxable wages are usually your gross pay minus pre-tax deductions like a traditional 401(k) and health insurance. That’s why your W-2 Box 1 is often lower than your YTD gross pay.

Is enough federal tax coming out?

Grab your latest stub and enter your YTD gross, YTD federal income tax, this check’s gross and federal tax, and your 401(k) and health deductions into my free withholding calculator. It shows whether you’re on track and how much extra to have withheld.

Your last pay stub of the year

Save it. It’s the best way to double-check your W-2 when it arrives. Your YTD federal income tax should match W-2 Box 2, and your YTD Social Security and Medicare should match Boxes 4 and 6.

See something wrong?

If your name, Social Security number, or filing status on file looks wrong, or federal tax isn’t being taken out when it should be, talk to your payroll or HR department right away. To change your withholding, turn in a new Form W-4.

Tips and overtime

For 2025 through 2028, qualified tips and the extra “half” of time-and-a-half overtime can be deducted. Keep your pay stubs showing tips and overtime; they back up the deduction when you file.

Tip: take a photo of your pay stub every few months and keep your last one of the year with your tax papers. It helps you and your tax preparer catch mistakes before you file.
