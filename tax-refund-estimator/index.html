Your household matters.

Count only children who meet the relationship, residency, support, dependent and citizenship rules and have a valid employment-authorized SSN by the return deadline. You, or at least one spouse on a joint return, must also have a valid SSN. Check the IRS child-credit rules. Do not enter SSNs here.

EITC child rules differ: generally under 19, or a full-time student under 24, or permanently and totally disabled, plus relationship, residency, joint-return and tiebreaker tests. A child released to a noncustodial parent does not automatically qualify that parent for EITC.

Review EITC requirements before selecting

Each filer and qualifying child must have a valid SSN by the filing deadline; filers must meet citizenship/residency rules and cannot be another person’s qualifying child. Children must meet every EITC test and cannot be claimed for EITC by someone else. With no qualifying children, you (or either spouse if joint) must be age 25–64, live in the U.S. for more than half the year, and not be someone else’s dependent. This tool assumes no investment income, no Form 2555, no EITC disallowance period and no special military, clergy or statutory-employee rules. Income limits apply automatically. Our calculation and IRS sources

2026 standard deductions: Single $16,100; Head of Household $24,150; Joint $32,200. Progressive 2026 rate schedules apply to income after the standard deduction and half-SE adjustment. Regular SE tax is 15.3% of 92.35% of positive net profit when net earnings reach $400. The household income limit keeps this simplified calculation below the Social Security wage base and Additional Medicare thresholds.

Eligible CTC is up to $2,200 per child; unused credit may qualify for ACTC, limited by earned income and $1,700 per child. The optional EITC uses 2026 maximums and phaseouts with statutory phase-in/phaseout percentages; official credit-table rounding may differ. A dependent count alone does not establish eligibility.
