Start with the Texas basics

Texas has no individual state income tax. Federal tax still matters.

Texas residents may still have federal income and self-employment tax responsibilities. If you have a business entity, separate state and federal filing rules may apply to that entity.

For many sole proprietors, business profit and expenses are reported as part of an individual federal return, commonly using Schedule C; self-employment tax is generally figured on Schedule SE when required. The forms and rules depend on your work, records, and business structure.

About Texas franchise tax: It is a business-entity tax, not a tax every 1099 worker automatically owes. Texas Comptroller guidance says a sole proprietorship that is not legally organized to limit liability is not a taxable entity; a single-member LLC may be a taxable entity even when it is treated as a sole proprietorship for federal income-tax purposes. D-XIRE prepares eligible individual returns and does not prepare separate business-entity returns. Ask a qualified business tax professional or the Comptroller about entity-specific reporting requirements.

Common self-employment tax topics.

Some costs may be deductible when they meet IRS rules and are supported by records. Whether a deduction applies depends on your circumstances.

Business use of home

Home office expenses

A home-office deduction may be available when a qualifying space is used regularly and exclusively for business, subject to IRS requirements and limits.

IRS: Business use of your home
Work-related driving

Business mileage

You may be able to use the standard mileage method or actual vehicle expenses, depending on eligibility, recordkeeping, and method-selection rules. Track business miles and keep supporting records.

IRS: Standard mileage rates
Tools & operations

Ordinary business costs

Advertising, software, supplies, platform costs, and similar expenses may qualify when they are ordinary and necessary for your business. Separate personal use and keep receipts.

IRS: Deducting business expenses
Health coverage

Self-employed health insurance

Some self-employed people may qualify to deduct premiums for themselves and eligible family members, subject to income, plan, and employer-coverage rules.
