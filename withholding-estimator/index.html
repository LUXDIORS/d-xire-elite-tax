Desire’s Tax Withholding Calculator

Use your latest paystub to check whether your current federal withholding may leave you with a balance due, and estimate an additional amount per remaining paycheck.

This D-XIRE tool uses IRS-style paystub inputs but is a simplified federal estimate, not the official IRS Tax Withholding Estimator or a tax return. It covers W-2 pay (including a spouse’s job), ordinary 1099/Schedule C income, and the new tips, overtime, and age 65+ deductions using 2026 rules. Your actual result can differ.

Optional: new deductions for tips, overtime, and age 65+

New federal deductions for 2025 through 2028 can lower the tax on tips and overtime. Leave these at $0 if they do not apply to you.

On your paystub, look under Earnings for “Tips,” “Cash tips,” or “Charged tips” YTD, then add what you expect through December 31. Count only tips you report, from a job where tipping is customary (servers, bartenders, hairstylists, nail techs, delivery drivers and similar). Up to $25,000 can be deducted. These tips should already be included in the pay or 1099 income you entered above.

On your paystub, look under Earnings for “Overtime” or “OT” YTD, then add what you expect through December 31. Enter your total overtime pay. The estimator counts only the extra “half” of time-and-a-half (one-third of your overtime pay), which is the part the deduction covers, up to $12,500 ($25,000 married filing jointly).

Count yourself, and your spouse if filing jointly. Each person 65+ may get an extra deduction of up to $6,000.

Your estimate at a glance

This estimate assumes the pay and withholding you entered continue for the remaining checks. The red or green box is the projected year-end outcome. The yellow box shows the estimated extra federal withholding per remaining W-2 paycheck when a balance is projected. Ask your employer about updating Form W-4 before changing paycheck withholding. For 1099 income, it estimates ordinary Schedule C profit and regular self-employment tax from the amounts you entered. It does not include bonuses or irregular pay, investment or retirement income, itemized deductions, education or other credits, special tax situations, or state tax. Total W-2 wages plus 1099 receipts are limited to $150,000 in this simplified model. For those situations, compare with the official IRS Tax Withholding Estimator or ask a tax professional.

How this estimate works

We project year-end gross W-2 pay and eligible pre-tax payroll deductions from year-to-date totals plus the remaining regular checks, then estimate federal taxable wages by subtracting those deductions from gross pay. If you enter federal taxable wages directly, leave the related deduction fields at $0. Federal withholding is projected separately. If you include 1099 income, the estimate subtracts entered business expenses from receipts, includes regular self-employment tax plus the half-SE-tax adjustment, and applies the 20% qualified business income deduction (with the 2026 $400 minimum when business income is at least $1,000). It applies the 2026 standard deduction, the optional tips (up to $25,000), overtime premium (up to $12,500, or $25,000 joint), and age 65+ ($6,000 per person) deductions, federal tax brackets, and the optional child and earned income credit inputs. A spouse’s job, when entered, is added to wages and withholding. If an estimated balance remains and you have W-2 paychecks left, the suggested additional withholding divides that amount evenly across those checks. To request it, enter the amount on Step 4(c) of a new Form W-4 and give it to your employer’s payroll or HR department.
